## Capacitors and derivatives

akuli-note:
    The discussion below uses derivatives.
    You don't really need to know how to define or calculate a derivative mathematically,
    but you need to have a **practical** grasp of what a derivative is.

    The tl;dr is that a derivative is the changing speed of something: it tells how fast a thing changes.
    Anything whose unit is "something per second" is likely a derivative.

    Examples:

    - When a car is moving, the derivative of the distance it has moved (metres) is the car's speed (metres/second). A positive derivative like 20m/s (also known as 72km/h or 44.7mph) means that the car moves forward. A negative derivative like -1m/s means that the car is reversing. Derivative being zero means that the car is not moving.
    - When downloading a file, the derivative of the amount of downloaded data (megabytes) is the download speed (megabytes/second). This derivative cannot be negative. Derivative being zero means that the downloading is stuck or paused.

    Mathematically, it is also possible to use something else instead of time,
    but that is rare in practice.
    Another way to say this, using fancier language,
    is that all the derivatives here are with respect to time.

    Below we use the derivative of a voltage. Its unit is volts/second.
